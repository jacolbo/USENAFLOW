Studio Retouching Guidelines: Achieving Client Satisfaction
Our Core Philosophy: Enhance, do not replace. Our goal is to make our clients look like the absolute best version of themselves on their best day—rested, vibrant, and confident. We never want a client to look at a photo and feel like they don't recognize themselves.

1. The "Standard Retouch" Rules
Unless a client has specifically requested heavy alterations or "glamour" editing, adhere strictly strictly to these baseline standards:

The Do's (Always fix these):

Temporary Blemishes: Remove acne, shaving cuts, unexpected scratches, or recent bruises. If it will be gone in two weeks, remove it today.

Distracting Elements: Clean up stray/flyaway hairs that cross the face or eyes, lint on clothing, and distracting background debris.

Skin Tone & Lighting: Even out minor skin discoloration (like mild under-eye shadows or redness) and ensure accurate, flattering color balance.

Teeth & Eyes: Apply subtle brightening to the teeth and the whites of the eyes. Do not over-whiten; they should not glow unnaturally.

The Don'ts (Never alter these without explicit written request):

Permanent Features: Never remove birthmarks, moles, freckles, or scars. These are part of a person's identity.

Body Modification: Do not digitally alter a client's weight, reshape their nose, slim their arms, or alter their jawline.

Age Erasing: Do not completely remove wrinkles or laugh lines. Soften them slightly if lighting has made them overly harsh, but do not give a 50-year-old the skin texture of a mannequin.

The "Plastic" Effect: Avoid heavy frequency separation or blurring that destroys natural skin pores and texture.

2. Workflow and Technical Standards
To ensure efficiency and protect original files, all retouchers must follow this workflow:

Non-Destructive Editing: Always work on duplicate layers or use adjustment layers. Never overwrite the original RAW or high-res JPEG files. * Monitor Calibration: Ensure your monitor is calibrated weekly. Color shifts between the editor's screen and the client's print/screen are a leading cause of complaints.

Zoom Out: It’s easy to get lost at 300% zoom. Frequently zoom out to 100% and 50% to ensure the overall image still looks natural and the edits aren't noticeable from a standard viewing distance.

File Naming: Adhere to the studio's naming conventions for final exports (e.g., ClientName_Date_Retouched_01.jpg) to prevent version confusion.

3. Handling Client Requests & Revisions
Sometimes clients will ask for edits that go against our natural philosophy. Here is how to handle them:

Follow the Client Notes: Always review the client's specific retouching requests before starting. If they asked for a mole to be removed, remove it.

When in Doubt, Ask: If a client requests something vague like "make me look skinnier," escalate this to the studio manager or client liaison to clarify exactly what the client means before spending hours on body-warping edits.

Revision Empathy: If a client requests a revision because they don't like how they look, do not take it personally. Validate their feelings, adjust the edit according to their feedback, and remember that self-image is deeply personal.

4. The Final Quality Assurance (QA) Checklist
Before marking an image as "Ready for Delivery," ask yourself:

[ ] Did I address all specific client notes?

[ ] Does the skin still have natural texture (pores)?

[ ] Are the eyes and teeth naturally bright, not glowing?

[ ] Is the background free of cloning stamps or smudges?

[ ] Would the client instantly recognize themselves?
